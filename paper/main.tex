\begin{abstract}
Gaussian noise injection is ubiquitous in representation learning, neural
operators, and diffusion models. Yet a noise level that is benign at one
resolution need not represent the same perturbation after refinement: its
relative covariance update can accumulate spectral mass in newly resolved
modes, causing Gaussian objectives and their gradients to be governed by the
discretization rather than signal geometry. We formulate this failure as a
continuum-stability problem. For a whitened covariance perturbation $K_N$, we
show that uniform Hilbert--Schmidt control is the sharp threshold for uniform
boundedness of Gaussian noise objectives under refinement and fixed spectral
bounds.
Trace-class perturbations admit ordinary trace and log-determinant formulas;
Hilbert--Schmidt but non-trace-class perturbations require the
Carleman--Fredholm determinant $\det_2$; beyond $\mathcal S_2$, this
second-order-renormalized objective loses uniform cutoff control. This
characterization yields
\emph{Schatten-filtered noise}, a spectral gate that explicitly constrains the
perturbation to a chosen operator-ideal regime. Synthetic power-law spectra
recover the predicted three-phase boundary, while resolution-scaling tests
measure objective drift, gradient variance, and noise-scale transfer. Finally,
we ask whether latent encoders act as implicit operator-ideal filters by
auditing both pushed-forward input perturbations and downstream diffusion
updates across resolutions. The resulting framework turns a hidden requirement of
high-dimensional noise injection into a falsifiable representation diagnostic
and a controllable noise-design principle.
\end{abstract}
