\section{Gaussian Noise Under Refinement}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Relative covariance and the finite certificate}

At cutoff $N$, compare
$p_N=\mathcal N(0,C_N)$ with
$q_N=\mathcal N(0,C_N+\Delta C_N)$ and whiten by the positive reference:
\begin{equation}
  K_N=C_N^{-1/2}\Delta C_NC_N^{-1/2},
  \qquad I+K_N\succ0.
  \label{eq:relative-covariance-cutoff}
\end{equation}
The covariance contribution to Gaussian KL is
\begin{equation}
  \KL(q_N\Vert p_N)
  =\frac12\sum_{j\le n_N}
    \bigl[\lambda_{j,N}-\log(1+\lambda_{j,N})\bigr].
  \label{eq:finite-gaussian-kl}
\end{equation}
Because the summand is quadratic near zero, define the operator functional and
its value along the cutoff sequence by
\begin{equation}
  \Ttwo(K):=\hsnorm{K}^{2},\qquad
  \Ttwo(N):=\Ttwo(K_N)
  =\Tr(K_N^\ast K_N)
  =\sum_{j\le n_N}s_j(K_N)^2.
  \label{eq:t2-definition}
\end{equation}
We report the finite certificate
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathfrak C_N&=(\Ttwo(N),M_N,\delta_N^{\mathrm{cert}}),
    &M_N&=\opnorm{K_N},\\
    \delta_N^{\mathrm{act}}&=\lambda_{\min}(I+K_N)>0,
    &\delta_N^{\mathrm{cert}}&=\min\{1,\delta_N^{\mathrm{act}}\}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:finite-certificate}
\end{equation}
Raw $\Ttwo$ sets the exact log-density variance scale, $M_N$ controls the
non-Gaussian tail, and $\delta_N^{\mathrm{cert}}$ supplies the theorem's
positivity constant; we also report the actual geometric margin
$\delta_N^{\mathrm{act}}$. All are dimensionless after whitening. A confidence
level completes the finite-risk statement in \cref{thm:finite-certificate}.

Every fixed matrix belongs to every finite-dimensional Schatten class; only a
sequence can reveal refinement instability. For example,
$C_N+\Delta C_N=aC_N$ gives
\begin{equation}
  K_N=(a-1)I_{n_N},
  \qquad \Ttwo(N)=(a-1)^2n_N,
  \label{eq:identity-direction}
\end{equation}
so an update that is legal at each cutoff accumulates in every new direction.
Likewise, $s_j(K)\asymp j^{-\alpha}$ has bounded squared mass only for
$\alpha>1/2$.

\subsection{Shell diagnostics}

A fitted decay exponent can hide alternating blocks, sparse spikes, or growing
multiplicity. We therefore report dyadic rank-shell mass
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal B^{\mathrm{rank}}_{N,\ell}
  :=\sum_{2^\ell\le j<\min\{2^{\ell+1},n_N+1\}}s_j(K_N)^2.
  \label{eq:rank-shell-mass}
\end{equation}
When a refinement-compatible physical decomposition exists, with
$P_{N,\ell}$ projecting onto
$2^\ell\le\lVert k\rVert<2^{\ell+1}$, we separately use
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal B^{\mathrm{phys}}_{N,\ell}
  :=\lVert K_NP_{N,\ell}\rVert_{\Schatten{2}}^2,
  \qquad
  \Ttwo(N)=\sum_\ell\mathcal B^{\mathrm{phys}}_{N,\ell}.
  \label{eq:physical-shell-mass}
\end{equation}
Rank is not spatial frequency: the first diagnostic is basis invariant, while
the second asks where physical modes enter. Growth slopes are always labeled by
their independent variable ($\log n_N$ or $\log R$).

If a continuum interpretation is claimed, the cutoffs must be compatible
subspaces of one Hilbert space, the reference covariance must itself be
positive, injective, and trace class, and the lifted updates must share a
positivity gap. These conditions, the precision--covariance conversion, and the
difference between boundedness and convergence are collected in
\cref{app:continuum-realization,app:precision-bridge}. Finite neural-feature
experiments use the diagnostics without asserting a white-noise continuum law.
