\section{Carleman--Fredholm Finite Part}
\label{app:det2}

For a finite self-adjoint matrix $K$ with $I+K\succ0$, define
\begin{equation}
  \det\nolimits_2(I+K):=\det(I+K)e^{-\Tr K}.
\end{equation}
The finite covariance objective in \cref{eq:renormalized-objective} is exactly
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L^{\mathrm{ren}}(K)
  =-\frac12\log\det\nolimits_2(I+K)
  =\frac12\sum_j\bigl[\lambda_j-\log(1+\lambda_j)\bigr].
  \label{eq:det2-finite-cutoff}
\end{equation}
This identity removes the first-order trace term; it does not alter any
finite-dimensional probability law. Its role becomes substantive under
refinement. If $K\in\Schatten{2}$ and $I+K\succeq\delta I$, the canonical
product
\begin{equation}
  \det\nolimits_2(I+K)
  =\prod_j(1+\lambda_j)e^{-\lambda_j}
\end{equation}
converges even when $K\notin\Schatten{1}$. Thus the ordinary trace and
log-determinant need not exist separately, whereas their finite part does.
If $K\in\Schatten{1}$, \cref{eq:det2-finite-cutoff} agrees with their usual
difference.

When $\opnorm{K}<1$, the logarithm has the absolutely convergent expansion
\begin{equation}
  -\log\det\nolimits_2(I+K)
  =\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k}{k}\Tr(K^k).
  \label{eq:det2-series}
\end{equation}
If $\opnorm{K}\le\rho<1$, truncation after order $L\ge2$ obeys
\begin{equation}
  \left|
    -\log\det\nolimits_2(I+K)
    -\sum_{k=2}^{L}\frac{(-1)^k}{k}\Tr(K^k)
  \right|
  \le
  \frac{\rho^{L-1}}{(L+1)(1-\rho)}\hsnorm{K}^{2}.
  \label{eq:det2-truncation}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $|\Tr(K^k)|\le\rho^{k-2}\hsnorm{K}^2$ and summing the geometric
tail proves the claim. The main text uses the raw certificate
$\bigl(\hsnorm{K}^2,\opnorm{K},
\min\{1,\lambda_{\min}(I+K)\}\bigr)$ and reports the unclipped geometric
margin separately; it does not require numerically evaluating
\cref{eq:det2-series}.


\section{Proofs for the Finite Certificate}
\label{app:finite-certificate-proof}

Let $g(\lambda)=\lambda-\log(1+\lambda)$. Taylor's theorem in integral form
gives, for every $\lambda>-1$,
\begin{equation}
  g(\lambda)
  =\lambda^2\int_0^1
    \frac{1-s}{(1+s\lambda)^2}\,ds.
  \label{eq:g-integral-representation}
\end{equation}
Under the assumptions of \cref{thm:finite-certificate}, every eigenvalue lies
in $[-1+\delta,M]$, so
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\lambda^2}{2(1+M)^2}
  \le g(\lambda)
  \le \frac{\lambda^2}{2\delta^2}.
\end{equation}
Summing and multiplying by $1/2$ proves
\cref{eq:kl-t2-equivalence}. If $K\succeq0$, then
\begin{equation}
  g(\lambda)=\int_0^\lambda\frac{t}{1+t}\,dt,
  \qquad
  \frac{\lambda^2}{2(1+M)}
  \le g(\lambda)\le\frac{\lambda^2}{2},
\end{equation}
which gives the sharpened constants.

We next prove the exact fluctuation and its high-probability version. Work in
whitened coordinates and diagonalize $K$. For
$X=(I+K)^{1/2}\xi$ with $\xi\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$,
\begin{equation}
  Y:=\log\frac{dq}{dp}(X)
    -\E_q\log\frac{dq}{dp}(X)
  =\frac12\sum_j\lambda_j(\xi_j^2-1).
  \label{eq:centered-log-rn-chaos}
\end{equation}
Independence and $\Var(\xi_j^2)=2$ give
$\Var(Y)=\hsnorm{K}^2/2$. Moreover, for $|t|<1/M$,
\begin{align}
  \log\E e^{tY}
  &=\frac12\sum_j\bigl[-\log(1-t\lambda_j)-t\lambda_j\bigr] \\
  &\le
    \frac{t^2\hsnorm{K}^2}{4(1-|t|M)}.
  \label{eq:centered-log-rn-mgf}
\end{align}
The inequality follows by expanding each term and using
$|\lambda_j|^k\le M^{k-2}\lambda_j^2$ for $k\ge2$. Applying the same bound
to $Y$ and $-Y$, followed by the Chernoff bound for a sub-gamma variable with
variance factor $\hsnorm{K}^2/2$ and scale $M$, yields
\begin{equation}
  \Pr\!\left(
    |Y|\ge\sqrt{\hsnorm{K}^2x}+Mx
  \right)\le2e^{-x}.
\end{equation}
Substituting $x=\log(2/\eta)$ proves
the explicit radius
\begin{equation}
  |Y|
  \le
  \sqrt{\hsnorm{K}^2\log\frac{2}{\eta}}
  +M\log\frac{2}{\eta}
  \qquad\text{with probability at least }1-\eta.
  \label{eq:finite-certificate-radius}
\end{equation}

The equivalences in \cref{thm:s2-stability} now follow immediately from the
two-sided objective bound and the exact variance identity, with the constants
uniform in $N$. The divergence statement follows from the corresponding
lower bounds. Finally, \cref{cor:power-law} is the integral-test asymptotic
for $\sum_{j\le n_N}j^{-2\alpha}$. These results establish boundedness under
refinement; they do not identify a continuum limit without compatibility.


\section{Compatible Continuum Realization}
\label{app:continuum-realization}

Let $\mathcal H$ be a separable real Hilbert space, let $C$ be positive,
injective, and trace class, and let $K=K^\ast\in\Schatten{2}$ satisfy
$I+K\succeq\delta I$. Since $K$ is bounded,
$C^{1/2}(I+K)C^{1/2}$ is trace class and defines a Gaussian covariance. The
uniform positivity gap makes its Cameron--Martin norm equivalent to that of
$C$, while the relative covariance update is Hilbert--Schmidt; the two
Gaussian measures are therefore equivalent.

Suppose compatible finite-rank lifts satisfy $K_N\to K$ in $\Schatten{2}$
and share the positivity gap. Continuity of $\det_2$ and an isonormal
coupling give
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_N^{\mathrm{ren}}\to
  -\frac12\log\det\nolimits_2(I+K),
  \qquad
  \E|Y_N-Y|^2=\frac12\hsnorm{K_N-K}^2\to0.
  \label{eq:compatible-cutoff-convergence}
\end{equation}
Uniform boundedness of $\hsnorm{K_N}$ without $\Schatten{2}$ convergence
gives the no-divergence statement of \cref{thm:s2-stability}, but it does not
assert convergence of an incompatible cutoff sequence.

\begin{corollary}[Power-law phase transition]
\label{cor:power-law}
If $s_j(K)\asymp j^{-\alpha}$ and $K_N$ retains the first $n_N$ singular
directions under the uniform conditions of \cref{thm:s2-stability}, then
\begin{equation}
  \Ttwo(N)\asymp
  \begin{cases}
    1, & \alpha>\tfrac12,\\
    \log n_N, & \alpha=\tfrac12,\\
    n_N^{1-2\alpha}, & \alpha<\tfrac12.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:power-law-phase-transition}
\end{equation}
This is an integral-test asymptotic; finite audits still report
$\Ttwo(N_{\max})$ and the independent variable used for any fitted slope.
\end{corollary}


\section{Precision--Covariance Bridge}
\label{app:precision-bridge}

Suppose $H=H^\ast$, $\opnorm{H}\le\rho<1$, and
$K=H(I-H)^{-1}$. Functional calculus gives
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{1+\rho}\hsnorm{H}
  \le \hsnorm{K}
  \le \frac{1}{1-\rho}\hsnorm{H}.
  \label{eq:precision-covariance-s2-bounds}
\end{equation}
Thus the two parameterizations have the same dimension-uniform
$\Schatten{2}$ boundary. If
$p=\mathcal N(0,C)$ and
$q=\mathcal N(0,C^{1/2}(I-H)^{-1}C^{1/2})$, then
\begin{align}
  \KL(q\Vert p)
  &=\frac12\left[\Tr K+\log\det(I-H)\right]
    =-\frac12\log\det\nolimits_2(I+K), \\
  \KL(p\Vert q)
  &=-\frac12\left[\Tr H+\log\det(I-H)\right]
    =-\frac12\log\det\nolimits_2(I-H).
  \label{eq:precision-two-kl-directions}
\end{align}
For $Z\sim p$ in whitened coordinates,
\begin{equation}
  \log\frac{dq}{dp}(Z)-\E_p\log\frac{dq}{dp}(Z)
  =\frac12\left(\langle Z,HZ\rangle-\Tr H\right),
  \qquad
  \Var_p\!\left[\log\frac{dq}{dp}(Z)\right]
  =\frac12\hsnorm{H}^2.
\end{equation}
Under $q$, the centered quadratic is instead governed by $K$ and has variance
$\hsnorm{K}^2/2$. This specifies both the sampling measure and the KL
orientation for precision-space implementations.


\section{Task-Aware Projection}
\label{app:task-aware-kkt}

The program in \cref{eq:task-aware-projection} is a convex quadratic program.
With multiplier $\mu/2\ge0$ for the squared Hilbert--Schmidt budget, its
separable Lagrangian (before imposing the box) is
\begin{equation}
  \frac12\sum_j q_j(\widetilde\lambda_j-\lambda_j)^2
  +\frac\mu2\left(\sum_j\widetilde\lambda_j^2-\tau\right).
\end{equation}
Stationarity gives
$(q_j+\mu)\widetilde\lambda_j=q_j\lambda_j$. Since
$q_j,\lambda_j\ge0$, projection onto $[0,\rho_{\mathrm{cap}}]$ therefore yields
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde\lambda_j(\mu)
  =\min\!\left\{\rho_{\mathrm{cap}},
    \frac{q_j}{q_j+\mu}\lambda_j
  \right\}.
\end{equation}
At $q_j=\mu=0$ the objective is flat in that coordinate; choosing zero gives
a deterministic minimum-budget solution. If the $\mu=0$ solution is
feasible, complementary slackness selects it. Otherwise
$S(\mu)=\sum_j\widetilde\lambda_j(\mu)^2$ is continuous and decreases from a
value above $\tau$ to zero, so one-dimensional bisection finds the unique
budget-active solution (with the $\tau=0$ case understood as the limit
$\mu\to\infty$). This derivation relies on the fixed commuting basis and a
positive-semidefinite quadratic task metric; it does not apply unchanged to
a learned basis or to an indefinite first-order score.

For a fixed compatible basis, a nonnegative envelope $w_j$ satisfying
$\sum_jw_j^2\le1$ gives the task-agnostic modewise alternative
\begin{equation}
  \phi_{\tau,j}(\lambda)
  =\min\{\lambda,\sqrt\tau\,w_j\},
  \qquad \lambda\ge0,
  \label{eq:envelope-gate}
\end{equation}
which also enforces squared Hilbert--Schmidt mass at most $\tau$. After any
covariance gate, sampling uses a factor
$B^{(\tau)}(B^{(\tau)})^\ast=K^{(\tau)}$:
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I)\text{ independently of }z,\qquad
  \xi^{(\tau)}=C^{1/2}B^{(\tau)}\epsilon,\qquad
  z'=z+\xi^{(\tau)}.
  \label{eq:noise-layer-interface}
\end{equation}


\section{Temporal Budget Identities}
\label{app:temporal-budgets}

For Markov laws with the required absolute continuity, factorization of their
joint densities and iterated expectation give
\begin{equation}
  \log\frac{dq_{0:T}}{dp_{0:T}}
  =\log\frac{dq_0}{dp_0}
   +\sum_{t=1}^T
    \log\frac{dq_t(\,\cdot\mid X_{t-1})}
             {dp_t(\,\cdot\mid X_{t-1})},
  \label{eq:path-kl-chain-rule}
\end{equation}
which proves \cref{eq:path-kl-chain-rule}. Conditional on
$\mathcal F_{t-1}$, equal-mean Gaussian transitions have covariance KL
$\mathcal L^{\mathrm{ren}}(K_t)$. Applying
\cref{thm:finite-certificate} conditionally and then taking expectation
proves the two-sided path-KL bound by fixed multiples of
$\mathcal B_{\mathrm{path}}$. If the transition means differ, the additional
term is
\begin{equation}
  \frac12(m_t^q-m_t^p)^\ast(C_t^p)^{-1}(m_t^q-m_t^p),
\end{equation}
and is outside the covariance certificate.

Let $\ell_t$ denote the conditional covariance log-ratio and
\begin{equation}
  d_t=\ell_t-\E_q[\ell_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}].
\end{equation}
Then $(d_t)$ is a martingale-difference sequence. Conditional application of
\cref{eq:centered-log-rn-chaos} yields
\begin{equation}
  \E_q[d_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
  =\frac12\Ttwo(K_t).
\end{equation}
Orthogonality of martingale differences proves
\begin{equation}
  \Var_q\!\left(\sum_{t=1}^Td_t\right)
  =\frac12\sum_{t=1}^T\E_q\Ttwo(K_t)
  =\frac12\mathcal B_{\mathrm{path}}.
\end{equation}
For deterministic weights, the corresponding variance is
$\frac12\sum_t w_t^2\E_q\Ttwo(K_t)$. This square on $w_t$ is distinct from
the linear weighting in a training loss. Raw increments $\ell_t$ are not in
general martingale differences, and state-dependent or data-dependent weights
require the corresponding predictable-weight formulation.

The endpoint budget is different again. For a linear Gaussian recursion
$X_t=A_tX_{t-1}+\epsilon_t$ with innovation covariance $Q_t$, for example,
\begin{equation}
  C_t=A_tC_{t-1}A_t^\ast+Q_t.
\end{equation}
One must run this recursion under both laws and whiten their final covariance
difference as in \cref{eq:endpoint-t2-budget}. Neither the path chain rule nor
the martingale variance identity turns this endpoint covariance into a sum or
product of stepwise relative updates.


\section{Encoder Audit and Coordinate Invariance}
\label{app:encoder-audit}

For $F$ of finite rank with singular values $s_j(F)$,
\begin{equation}
  \hsnorm{FF^\ast}^{2}
  =\Tr\bigl((FF^\ast)^2\bigr)
  =\Tr\bigl((F^\ast F)^2\bigr)
  =\sum_j s_j(F)^4.
\end{equation}
This proves \cref{eq:encoder-s4-identity}. It also explains why an
operator-norm cap alone is insufficient: if $r=\operatorname{rank}(F)$, then
\begin{equation}
  \lVert F\rVert_{\Schatten{4}}^4
  \le r\opnorm{F}^4,
\end{equation}
so growing effective rank can consume the squared Hilbert--Schmidt budget even
when the largest singular value is controlled.

For an invertible raw-coordinate adapter $A$, let
\begin{equation}
  C'=ACA^\ast,\qquad
  F'=(C')^{-1/2}AJ\Sigma^{1/2}
     =QF,\qquad
  Q=(C')^{-1/2}AC^{1/2}.
  \label{eq:encoder-coordinate-change}
\end{equation}
Direct multiplication gives
\begin{equation}
  QQ^\ast=(C')^{-1/2}ACA^\ast(C')^{-1/2}=I.
\end{equation}
In equal finite dimensions with invertible $A$, $Q$ is unitary; hence $QF$ and
$F$ have identical singular values. A genuine repair must therefore modify
the noise covariance in one declared whitening geometry, as in
\cref{eq:fixed-whitened-latent-gate}, or change the represented support. If
the reference is re-estimated after fitting the gate, the intervention and
audit geometry change together and the certificate must be recomputed.


\section{Matrix-Free Estimators and Probe Cost}
\label{app:estimators}

Let $A=K^\ast K$. For an unnormalized real Rademacher probe $v$,
$\lVert Kv\rVert^2=v^\top Av$ and
\begin{equation}
  \E[v^\top Av]=\Tr A=\hsnorm{K}^2,
  \qquad
  \Var(v^\top Av)=2\sum_{i\ne j}A_{ij}^2.
  \label{eq:hutchinson-exact-variance}
\end{equation}
Averaging $m$ independent probes divides this variance by $m$. Consequently
probe count is governed by off-diagonal mass in the probe basis and the desired
confidence width, not directly by model parameter count. For complex Fourier
operators, one may either use independent unit-circle probes with
$\E[vv^\ast]=I$ or realify the operator; the probe law and multiplicities must
be recorded.

Structured diagonal and low-rank updates should be summed exactly. For an
implicit encoder update $K=FF^\ast$, one application of $Kv$ requires one
application each of $F^\ast$ and $F$, which corresponds to a VJP and a JVP
together with the whitening and input-covariance maps. Differentiating this
quantity through encoder parameters may require second-order autodiff. A
truncated regularized determinant estimator uses $O(mL)$ operator--vector
products and has deterministic truncation error
\cref{eq:det2-truncation}; it is not required for the raw-$\Ttwo$ audit.

An online one-probe value is a stochastic training surrogate, not a finite
certificate. A final audit uses probes drawn independently of training and
fixed across paired methods. Across resolutions, a common bank is valid only
after specifying a coupling of compatible nested spaces. Optional stopping
requires a sequentially valid confidence procedure or a predeclared probe
batch schedule; repeatedly inspecting a fixed-$m$ confidence interval does
not preserve its nominal coverage. Common random numbers often improve a
paired difference empirically, but this is not guaranteed without controlling
the covariance of the two estimators. Finally, a blockwise or localized
surrogate certifies only that block; it does not inherit the global theorem.


\section{Frequency-Matched Controls}
\label{app:frequency-controls}

For completeness, let $d=m^2$ and define two nonnegative spectra by
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_j^{\mathrm{stable}}
  =\begin{cases}
    \eta, & j=1,\\
    \eta/(m+1), & 2\le j\le d,
  \end{cases}
  \qquad
  \lambda_j^{\mathrm{unstable}}
  =\begin{cases}
    \eta, & 1\le j\le m,\\
    0, & m<j\le d.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:appendix-matched-spectrum-pair}
\end{equation}
Let $U_d$ be a fixed flat unitary and set
$A_d^{(a)}=U_d\operatorname{diag}(\lambda^{(a)})U_d^\ast$ for
$a\in\{\mathrm{stable},\mathrm{unstable}\}$. A flat unitary has
constant-modulus entries, so each diagonal entry of
$U_d\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)U_d^\ast$ equals
$d^{-1}\sum_j\lambda_j$. Both spectra have trace $\eta m$, giving the shared
diagonal $\eta/m$. Their largest eigenvalue is $\eta$, while direct summation
gives
\begin{align}
  \sum_j\bigl(\lambda_j^{\mathrm{stable}}\bigr)^2
  &=\eta^2\left[1+\frac{m^2-1}{(m+1)^2}\right]
    =\eta^2\frac{2m}{m+1}, \\
  \sum_j\bigl(\lambda_j^{\mathrm{unstable}}\bigr)^2
  &=m\eta^2.
\end{align}
Thus the stable family has bounded Hilbert--Schmidt mass but diverging trace,
whereas the unstable family leaves $\Schatten{2}$.

To match spatial-frequency appearance, let
\begin{equation}
  G_R=\operatorname{diag}\left[
    (1+\lVert k\rVert^2)^{-s/2}
  \right]_{\lVert k\rVert\le R},
  \qquad s>1,
\end{equation}
and set $K_{R,d}^{(\cdot)}=G_R\otimes A_d^{(\cdot)}$. The two families have
the same Fourier diagonal and hence the same radial power summary. For every
spatial mask $M_R$,
\begin{equation}
  \hsnorm{(M_RG_RM_R)\otimes A_d^{(\cdot)}}^2
  =\hsnorm{M_RG_RM_R}^2\hsnorm{A_d^{(\cdot)}}^2.
\end{equation}
Any frequency-only control that retains a nonzero low-frequency response has
$\hsnorm{M_RG_RM_R}\ge c>0$ and therefore preserves the unstable family's
$\sqrt d$ divergence. The comparison matches Fourier diagonal, radial power,
trace, operator norm, bandwidth, low-frequency response, parameter count,
probe count, and compute budget. Closed-form spectra provide the ground truth;
Hutchinson probes only validate the matrix-free estimator.


\section{Additional Experimental Details}
\label{app:experimental-details}

\paragraph{Resolution and covariance contract.}
Each natural image and controlled sample is formed once at the highest
resolution and restricted with one fixed antialiasing operator. Split
membership, augmentations, mode labels, and random seeds are shared across the
ladder. Adjoint operators use the quadrature-weighted inner product of their
discretization. At every intervention, $B_R$ denotes a noise amplitude and
$K_R=B_RB_R^\ast$ the whitened relative covariance. We report
$\Ttwo(R)=\Tr(K_R^\ast K_R)$ without division by pixels, channels, latent
dimension, or effective rank; a diagonal amplitude $b_j$ therefore contributes
$b_j^4$. Rank shells and physical-frequency shells are never conflated.

\paragraph{Final probes and paired comparisons.}
Diagonal and explicitly low-rank operators are summed exactly. For an implicit
operator, a disjoint calibration split selects the final probe count from a
predeclared grid using absolute and relative precision targets. The selected
count, support regularization, whitening, and estimator hyperparameters are
then frozen. A public seed manifest generates one audit bank; compatible nested
spaces share probe signs on common modes, and every baseline, budget, seed, and
time uses the same bank. Training uses a separate fresh one-probe stream and
never observes final probes. We will validate the estimator against exact
materialization at the smallest feasible cutoff and report probe uncertainty
separately from image and adaptation-seed variation.

For an implicit Gaussian comparison, we set
$\eta_{\mathrm{est}}=0.025$ for the probe upper-confidence bound and
$\eta_{\mathrm{tail}}=0.025$ for the conditional log-density tail, giving
total failure probability at most $0.05$ by a union bound. Structured exact
comparisons use the same $\eta_{\mathrm{tail}}$ and have no probe error.
These probabilities are per audited operator. Any simultaneous statement over
the full resolution--time--arm grid uses a predeclared Bonferroni allocation
and displays the adjusted radii.
Positive injected-covariance operators have actual margin at least one, hence
$\delta^{\mathrm{cert}}=1$; when no tighter certified cap is available, the tail radius uses
$M\le\hsnorm K=\sqrt{\Ttwo(K)}$ with the upper confidence bound substituted.
A signed operator receives a tail certificate only when a valid lower
positivity bound is available; otherwise $(M,\delta^{\mathrm{act}})$ is explicitly labeled a
numerical diagnostic.

All learned comparisons match data, update count, optimizer, adapter parameter
count, and three adaptation seeds. Confidence intervals use paired examples and
common random numbers. Primary quality, texture quality, raw $\Ttwo$, and cost
remain separate axes rather than being collapsed into a single score. The
quality non-inferiority margins and maximum audit-cost multiplier are stored in
the locked configuration before the main seeds. Every audit reports
operator--vector products, backward equivalents, wall-clock time, and peak
accelerator memory.

\paragraph{Controlled mechanism test.}
The controlled families include alternating dyadic blocks with decay exponents
$0.4$ and $0.6$, a front-heavy spectrum with a light tail, a rapidly decaying
tail with sparse spikes, and the multiplicity construction in
\cref{app:frequency-controls}. Exact cumulative mass is the membership
decision; no fitted exponent replaces it. Conditional samples are generated on
the finest Fourier grid. The binary label changes the covariance on a fixed
sparse set of high modes below the Nyquist frequency of every reported cutoff;
low modes have the same nuisance law in both classes. A four-layer convolutional
classifier with global pooling is trained on samples from the unmodified
base-resolution generator and frozen. Squared sensitivities of conditional loss
to covariance-eigenvalue deviations are estimated on the training split,
averaged within symmetry blocks, and frozen. All covariance gates are converted
back to amplitude factors before sampling. The same validation-locked $\tau$
grid is used for no intervention, scalar scaling, hard and smooth low-pass
filters, an operator-norm cap, radial projection, and task-aware projection.

\paragraph{Pinned latent-diffusion system.}
The real suite pins a Stable Diffusion v1.x-compatible pipeline with the
\texttt{stabilityai/sd-vae-ft-mse} encoder/decoder and its matching frozen
denoiser. Exact model revisions, scheduler configuration, preprocessing, latent
scaling, and data-manifest hashes are artifact inputs. The full stochastic VAE
pushforward includes both the encoder-mean Jacobian term and posterior
covariance from the law of total covariance. The clean encoder-mean covariance
is the latent reference and is estimated on the training split. One
sample-independent Fourier--channel basis is fixed at the largest resolution
and restricted to lower cutoffs. The task-aware KKT rule acts on the population
operator's diagonal structured component; a final radial factor enforces the
full-population budget on the finite adapter-training manifest at every $R$.
The empirical $\overline K_{E,R}^{\mathrm{full}}$, basis, reference, and radial
factor are then frozen. Held-out audit and test manifests estimate their own
population covariance only for evaluation; they never update or rescale the
gate. A held-out budget violation is reported as such. Per-image Schatten-$4$
values remain a separate distributional diagnostic.

For a finite empirical population
$\overline K=n^{-1}\sum_iK(x_i)$, a population Hutchinson probe first forms
$\overline u=n^{-1}\sum_iK(x_i)v$ and then records
$\lVert\overline u\rVert^2$. Averaging
$\lVert K(x_i)v\rVert^2$ over examples estimates a different pointwise
quantity and is used only when that quantity is explicitly requested.

For the real suite, define
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{task}}
  =\frac{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{LPIPS}}}
         {\overline{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{LPIPS}}^{0}}
   +\frac{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{denoise}}}
         {\overline{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{denoise}}^{0}},
  \label{eq:real-task-loss}
\end{equation}
where the denominators are unfiltered means on the adapter-training split.
For every mode, $q_j$ is the training-split average of the squared derivative
of \cref{eq:real-task-loss} with respect to its covariance eigenvalue. The
average uses a predeclared uniform draw over reported resolutions and the
pipeline's frozen training-time timestep law; a mode is averaged only over
resolutions where it exists. Values are averaged within conjugacy and channel
symmetry blocks and frozen before validation. Texture metrics are evaluation
guardrails and do not enter $q_j$.

In the adapted protocol only the covariance gate is updated; the encoder,
decoder, denoiser, basis, and reference remain fixed, so
all arms stay in the whitening geometry of
\cref{eq:fixed-whitened-latent-gate}. Any encoder-unfreezing ablation must
re-estimate its reference and is not used for the main claim.

Per-image $K_{E,R}^{\mathrm{full}}(x)$ values receive only the local
covariance-matched Gaussian proxy certificate defined in the main text. The
population mixture receives raw covariance diagnostics and no density-ratio
tail claim.

The texture-rich reconstruction subset is selected before training using
source-image high-band energy alone. The corresponding generation subset is
selected from prompts by a predeclared rule independent of generated images.
Operating points are selected on validation data as: best quality under a raw
$\Ttwo$ ceiling, best raw $\Ttwo$ within the quality margin, and the frontier
knee. No test metric participates in selection. For the temporal audit, one
fixed positive reference schedule $p$ is chosen before evaluation. Every gate
defines $q^g$ by adding its PSD innovation covariance to the same reference and
shares $q^g_0=p_0$ and the conditional mean map. Conditional $K^g_{R,t}$
defines maximum and path budgets. For the nonlinear denoiser,
$C^p_{R,T}$ and $C^{q^g}_{R,T}$ are estimated from paired endpoint samples
under the two full path laws, using the law of total covariance when
within-sample stochasticity is present. Their whitened difference gives the
endpoint covariance diagnostic. Analytic scheduler covariance recurrence is
used only for linear-Gaussian calibration, including constant and idealized
variance-preserving schedules.

\paragraph{Claim gates and artifact record.}
The controlled claim requires agreement between exact and stochastic raw
$\Ttwo$, separation of the frequency-matched pair, and a nondominated
task-aware frontier segment that retains the high-frequency signal. The encoder
claim requires a nondominated segment under both main and texture-quality
margins. The temporal claim additionally requires paired
$\mathcal B_{\mathrm{path}}$ and $\mathcal B_{\mathrm{end}}$ improvement at
matched generation quality; a single-step reduction is insufficient. A cost
claim additionally requires the locked time and memory caps. A failed gate
removes that claim without changing the test protocol.

The final artifact will record all resolution ladders, splits, seeds, confidence
intervals, hyperparameters, discretization weights, covariance references,
probe banks, scheduler recurrences, raw outputs, figure commands, and compute
measurements.
