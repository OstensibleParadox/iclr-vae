\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{Noise and spectral control.}
Dropout uses stochastic feature perturbations~\citep{srivastava2014dropout},
while spectral normalization bounds the largest singular value of a learned
map~\citep{miyato2018spectral}. Diffusion work has adjusted schedules for high
resolution~\citep{hoogeboom2023simple}, learned multivariate noise
\citep{sahoo2024adaptive}, reweighted timesteps by signal-to-noise ratio
\citep{hang2023minsnr}, and designed schedules from frequency response
\citep{benita2025spectral}. These approaches change complementary parts of the
pipeline. Our constraint instead targets cumulative squared mass of a
\emph{whitened relative covariance} under resolution and time, and its
task-aware allocation need not favor low frequency.

\paragraph{Latent and function-space diffusion.}
Latent diffusion~\citep{rombach2022high} applies Gaussian updates in an
autoencoded representation~\citep{kingma2014autoencoding}, reducing the cost of
pixel-space diffusion~\citep{ho2020denoising}. We treat the encoder's spectral
effect as measurable rather than automatic: low finite dimension and good
reconstruction do not imply uniform control along refinement. Function-space
diffusion and neural operators study resolution-compatible laws
\citep{kerrigan2023diffusion,seidman2023variational}, while regularized
function-space objectives address well-posedness
\citep{cinquin2025well}. Gaussian equivalence and the Hilbert--Carleman
determinant identify Hilbert--Schmidt relative updates as the asymptotic domain
\citep{bogachev1998gaussian,minh2022kullback}; our focus is the finite budget
and the task decision inside that domain.
